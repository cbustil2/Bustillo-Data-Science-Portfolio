# Introduction to Data Science

In my junior year, I took an introduction to data science during the spring sememster. The course provided an overview of data science from both a computer and social science perspective. As a data science minor, this class helps provide me a baselevel understanding of the field of study, consisting with coding, analyzing data, and machine learning models. Below is a quick summary of what I learned from each week. 

## Week 1: Foundations of Data Science and Career Discernment

This was an introduction to the core ideas that define data science. This helped me ask myself questions like: how is data collected, structured, analyzed, and used to support decisions. I was able to explore and learn more about the major roles in the data ecosystem—analyst, engineer, scientist—and begin to familiarize with which paths align with my interests, strengths, and long‑term goals.

## Week 2: Github and Development Tools

During this week, I was introduced to GitHub. I created a profile and learned the importance of a well-updated, formatted, and outlined repository to express projects and apps. With GitHub, this has been a great source of finding other people who are interested in data science or has done similar projects. 

## Week 3: Data Storytelling & Streamlit

After reading an article on storytelling, I learn how data can be appropriately used to explain a particular event or situation. I learn the do's and do not's in data storytelling. Something very important that I learned from the article and class is the importance of walking with the audience and ensuring they are at the same level as you are. I also was introduced to streamlit and learned the basics of deploying an app.

## Week 4: Data Storytelling & Streamlit (cont.)

Much like the previous week, focused more on developing an app. This week introduced importance of visuals and how data can be constructed into such to help interact with the user or observer. In class, we watched a very impactful video titled ["The Best Stats You've Ever Seen](https://www.ted.com/talks/hans_rosling_the_best_stats_you_ve_ever_seen). In the video, it taught me how important and moving a display of stats can have on the audience.


## Week 5: Handling Missing and Unclean Data

This week, we dove deep into data quality problems. I practiced identifying missingness patterns, cleaning inconsistent values, and applying strategies such as identifying, cleaning, and replacing data to prepare datasets for analysis.

## Week 6: Principles of Tidy Data and Data Transformation

Focusing more on tidying data, we moved from handling data to how to reshape, merge, filter, and aggregate datasets. The week centers on building reliable data pipelines and transforming raw data into analysis‑ready formats, such as pivot tables, that allows to see the important features and consolidate the data.
